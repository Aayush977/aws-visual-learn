Every other lesson on this site assumes you are building something new. Most real companies are
not — they have two hundred applications in a data centre with a lease expiring in eighteen
months, and the question is what to do with each one.

This was added to the Cloud Practitioner blueprint deliberately, and it is examined in two
halves: the **strategies** for moving an application, and the **framework** for organising the
whole programme.

## The 7 R's — one decision per application

You do not migrate a company. You migrate an application, two hundred times, and each time you
choose one strategy for it. They are ordered here by effort.

You will see these called **the 6 R's** in most study material, and AWS's own documentation now
lists **seven** — *Relocate* was added for VMware estates. Learn all seven; if a question offers
six, Relocate is simply the one left out.

<Callout type="exam">
The two most-confused pairs. <strong>Rehost</strong> changes nothing; <strong>replatform</strong>
makes a small optimisation on the way (the classic example is self-managed MySQL → RDS).
<strong>Repurchase</strong> means abandoning your application for a SaaS product;
<strong>refactor</strong> means rewriting it yourself. If the question says "without changing the
application", it is rehost.
</Callout>

## The Cloud Adoption Framework — six perspectives

The **AWS Cloud Adoption Framework (CAF)** is about the *organisation*, not the servers. It
splits the work of adopting cloud into six **perspectives**, and the exam expects you to know
that the first two are business-facing and the last four are technical.

| Perspective | Owned by | Concerned with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Business** | Executives, finance | Does this create measurable business value? |
| **People** | HR, team leads | Skills, roles, culture, training the staff you already have |
| **Governance** | Programme, finance | Managing the portfolio, cost control, measuring benefit |
| **Platform** | Architects, engineers | Building the target environment and patterns |
| **Security** | Security, compliance | Meeting objectives for confidentiality, integrity, compliance |
| **Operations** | Operations teams | Running and recovering workloads to agreed service levels |

<Callout type="gotcha">
The first two perspectives — <strong>Business and People</strong> — are the ones migrations
actually fail on, and the ones people forget in the exam because they are not technical. If a
question describes a skills gap or a team that resists the move, that is the
<strong>People</strong> perspective, not Platform.
</Callout>

CAF also describes the migration itself in three phases: **Assess** (portfolio discovery and
business case), **Mobilise** (build the landing zone, close skill gaps, pilot), and
**Migrate and Modernise** (move at scale).

## The services that actually move the data

Recognise these by the constraint in the question:

| Service | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| **AWS Migration Hub** | The single dashboard tracking progress of a migration across tools and Regions |
| **AWS Application Migration Service (MGN)** | The primary **rehost** tool — replicates servers and cuts over with minimal downtime |
| **AWS Database Migration Service (DMS)** | Moves databases, with the **source staying online** during the migration |
| **AWS Schema Conversion Tool (SCT)** | Converts schema and code when the engine changes, e.g. Oracle → Aurora PostgreSQL |
| **AWS DataSync** | Automated transfer of file data **over the network** to S3, EFS or FSx |
| **AWS Storage Gateway** | Hybrid — keeps on-premises applications working while data lives in AWS |
| **AWS Snowball / Snowmobile** | Physical devices for **very large** transfers where the network would take too long |
| **AWS Transfer Family** | Managed SFTP/FTPS/FTP into S3 |

<Callout type="exam">
The Snow family question is always about <strong>time and bandwidth</strong>, not size alone. If
the scenario mentions petabytes, a slow or metered connection, or "would take months over the
network", the answer is <strong>Snowball</strong>. If the link is decent and the transfer is
ongoing rather than one-off, it is <strong>DataSync</strong>.
</Callout>

<Callout type="cost">
DMS plus SCT is the standard pairing for a <em>heterogeneous</em> migration — one where the
database engine changes. If the engine stays the same (Oracle to Oracle), DMS alone is enough
and SCT is an unnecessary answer.
</Callout>

<Scenario
  who="An insurer closing a data centre in fourteen months"
  problem="Two hundred and forty applications, one hard deadline, and a team who had never used AWS. The first instinct was to modernise everything onto containers — an estimate that came back at four years. Meanwhile 80 TB of claim documents needed to move over a connection that would have taken about seven months to carry them."
  solution="A portfolio assessment retired 41 applications nobody used and retained 12 for licensing reasons. The remaining 187 were mostly rehosted with Application Migration Service, tracked in Migration Hub. Nineteen were replatformed — self-managed databases moved to RDS using DMS, which kept the source online during the cutover. Two customer-facing applications were refactored, because scale was the actual business problem. The 80 TB of documents went on Snowball devices."
  outcome="The data centre closed with six weeks to spare. Roughly a fifth of the estate never moved at all, and the two refactored applications delivered most of the measured business benefit."
>
The shape here is the exam's model answer: <strong>retire and retain first</strong> (they are
free), <strong>rehost the bulk</strong> against a deadline, and <strong>refactor only where the
business case is real</strong>. Optimisation comes after the lease expires, not before.
</Scenario>
