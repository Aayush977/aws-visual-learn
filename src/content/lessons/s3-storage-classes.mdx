S3 storage classes look like seven separate products. They are closer to one product
with a dial on it, and the dial is labelled **how long are you willing to wait?**

Turn it towards "instantly" and storage costs more. Turn it towards "some time
tomorrow" and storage gets very cheap, but getting the object back starts costing money
and time. Durability does not change: every class except One Zone-IA stores your object
across at least three Availability Zones, and all of them are designed for the same
99.999999999% (eleven nines) durability.

## The table the exam is testing

| Class | Retrieval time | AZs | Minimum duration | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Standard** | milliseconds | ≥ 3 | none | Active data, websites, content distribution |
| **Intelligent-Tiering** | milliseconds | ≥ 3 | none | Data whose access pattern you cannot predict |
| **Standard-IA** | milliseconds | ≥ 3 | 30 days | Backups and older data you occasionally need at once |
| **One Zone-IA** | milliseconds | **1** | 30 days | Data you could regenerate — thumbnails, secondary copies |
| **Glacier Instant Retrieval** | milliseconds | ≥ 3 | 90 days | Archives read a few times a year but needed immediately |
| **Glacier Flexible Retrieval** | 1 min – 12 h | ≥ 3 | 90 days | Backups and archives where waiting is acceptable |
| **Glacier Deep Archive** | 12 – 48 h | ≥ 3 | 180 days | Seven-year compliance retention nobody expects to read |

<Callout type="cost">
The **minimum storage duration** is the trap. Put a file in Standard-IA and delete it
after a week, and you are still billed for thirty days. Put it in Deep Archive and
delete it after a month, and you are billed for six. Cold storage is only cheap for
data that is genuinely going to sit still.
</Callout>

<Callout type="gotcha">
**One Zone-IA** stores data in a single Availability Zone. Its durability figure is the
same eleven nines *within* that zone, but if the zone is destroyed, the data is gone.
Use it only for data you can recreate from somewhere else. If a question says "must
survive the loss of an Availability Zone", One Zone-IA is a wrong answer.
</Callout>

## Intelligent-Tiering deserves its own paragraph

Most classes ask you to predict the future. **S3 Intelligent-Tiering** does not: it
monitors access per object and moves each one between a frequent tier, an infrequent
tier and (optionally) archive tiers automatically. There is a small per-object
monitoring charge, and no retrieval fee for the automatic tiers.

The practical rule: if you *know* the access pattern, pick the specific class and pay
less. If you genuinely do not know, or it changes, Intelligent-Tiering costs less than
guessing wrong.

<Callout type="exam">
Any question containing "unknown", "unpredictable" or "changing access patterns"
alongside "without operational overhead" or "without application changes" is an
Intelligent-Tiering question.
</Callout>

## Lifecycle rules do the moving

You do not migrate objects by hand. A **lifecycle configuration** on the bucket says
things like "transition to Standard-IA after 30 days, to Glacier Flexible Retrieval
after 90, expire after 2555 days", and S3 applies it to every object that matches a
prefix or tag.

Two rules worth remembering:

- Transitions only move **downwards** in the diagram above — towards colder, cheaper
  storage. Moving an object back to a warmer class means restoring or copying it.
- An object must spend **at least 30 days** in Standard before a lifecycle rule can
  transition it to Standard-IA or One Zone-IA.
